## Sources

1. **Synthetic, labels by construction** (`src/jobx/data/`). A structured record is sampled first (company, role, seniority, skills, experience, salary, cities, work mode, employment type, education), then rendered to text through alias tables, one of 12 template families and noise operators. The label is the sampled record, so it is exact; no model labels anything.
2. **Paraphrases** of a training subset by an open 7B instruct model (notebook `01_paraphrase_7b`), kept only if the rule + NLI checker passes. **Pending GPU run**: not yet included.
3. **Real postings** from a public Hugging Face dataset for `test_real`, labelled by a strong-model draft plus human verification with `jobx verify`. **Pending**: see `docs/real_dataset.md` (licence check is a TODO).

Company names are fictional (compositional, e.g. stem + sector); any resemblance to a real company is coincidental. Phone numbers, e-mails and links in the text are random placeholders (`careers.example.in`).

## Known biases and limitations

- **Template artefacts.** However many families and noise operators, synthetic text has regularities a model can learn. That is why the headline number is on real postings.
- **Short texts.** Synthetic postings are shorter than many real job descriptions (median above); long real JDs with long boilerplate are under-represented.
- **Vocabulary coverage.** Skills, cities and degrees come from fixed alias tables (`src/jobx/vocab.py`). Skills outside the table are never in synthetic gold labels.
- **Salary bands** are plausible ranges by seniority for tech vs non-tech, not survey data. USD salaries appear only for a few remote roles and are converted at a fixed 83 INR/USD.
- **Metro skew** is deliberate (Bengaluru, Hyderabad, Pune, Chennai, NCR dominate) and mirrors job boards, so tier-2/3 cities are rarer.
- **Seniority** is labelled only when stated in words; it is never inferred from experience, so a model that infers it is penalised as hallucinating.
- **English-first.** Hinglish appears as fragments and one held-out family, not full Hindi postings.
